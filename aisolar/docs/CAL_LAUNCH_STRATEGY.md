# AISolar — Launch · Marketing · Sales Strategy
### Written by Hermes, 3 Aug 2026. A real strategy with a point of view, built from the launch docs (GO_LIVE, WEBSITE_INTEGRATION, LAUNCH_HANDOVER, ONBOARDING_SPEC, COMPLIANCE_CHAIN_DESIGN) and the build state. Not a transcript of today's chat.
### Scope: AISolar (the solar-installer OS) only. Kernel / GENNY / franchise architecture excluded per steer.

---

## 0. The strategic position (read this first)
The launch docs are honest about one thing we should repeat until it's instinct: **don't fight on breadth.** Against OpenSolar and the field-SaaS incumbents, AISolar loses on proposal-studio depth, field-app maturity, analytics, and integrations breadth. That's fine. Those companies *cannot* win the one thing that matters to an installer's actual week:

**AISolar runs the whole solar business and does the boring 90% for them — from the bill a customer uploads to the NC paperwork filed with ESB and SEAI — in one platform, mostly on autopilot.**

The competitor is a design tool or a proposal tool. AISolar is the **operating system of the business.** That is the wedge, and it is stated plainly, not technically:
- **AI bill reader** reads the upload — no manual entry, no typos, no "send me that again."
- **Automated estimate** drops out instantly: savings, grant, payback, from the customer's *real* day/night usage.
- **The callback books itself** — customer picks a slot, it's in the diary, the reminder goes. No chase.
- **One platform** — no five tabs (calculator, spreadsheet, email, calendar, form-filler). One login runs the job.
- **One maths chain** — bill reading → survey → proposal, all one engine, so the numbers can never disagree.
- **Automated follow-up** — quiet leads chased, proposal sent, deposit reminded; the installer only hears about leads that need a human.
- **In-field time back** — the installer opens one app: today's job, the route, the checklist, the serials. No clipboard, no "where's the address," no driving in circles.
- **NC paper trail assembles itself** for both ESB and SEAI — the cert read against what was typed, filed clean.

The benefit is **time and certainty.** The installer stops being a paper-pusher and a chase-artist. The AI does the reading, estimating, booking, following-up, routing, and form-assembling. The human does the roof, the relationship, and the signature.

The compliance trail (ESB + SEAI) is the **capstone and the trust proof** — it's the bit global tools genuinely can't do, so it's what makes the automation *believable*. But it is not the headline. The headline is: **"Your whole solar business, automated, in one platform — and you only show up for the human moments."** Sell the time back first; the clean NC filing is why they can trust it.

The corollary: **sell the day back, then sell the relief.** The day-one reason to sign up is "I get 6 hours of my week back starting Monday." The deeper reason to trust it is "and the paperwork files itself, correctly, for both ESB and SEAI." Everything else is supporting cast.

## 1. Who we sell to (and who we don't)
**Primary:** Irish solar installers — SMEs from a one-man operation to a twelve-crew company. The product's four-dimension tenant model was explicitly built so a one-man op and a multi-crew firm differ only in the person/job layers, not the company/product layers. That means the *same pitch* lands for both — only the onboarding depth changes.

**First cohort:** 10 hand-picked clients, concierge-onboarded by Cal personally. This is not a gap to apologise for — at 10 clients, personally setting up each installer *is* best practice and a feature. Do not build month-6 SaaS (dunning, seat policing, SSO, ticketing) before client #1. The notes are explicit: the trap is building month-6 SaaS before client #1.

**The launch shape is already verified live** (routing proven 8/8): Renewable Ireland + Solar Ireland (national, one door each) · Saunderson + Wide Awake Solar (independent) + Solar Roscommon (county, franchised) = the first client's three brands on one tenant · Tyrone/Westmeath held. We are not launching blind — the deployment shape exists and routes correctly.

**Who we don't chase at launch:** enterprise, local-authority, or agri-voltaic at scale. Commercial (NDMG) is supported by the engine but the sales motion is SME installers. Stay in the lane.

## 2. The message (hierarchy, not menu)
1. **Primary (lead with this):** "Your whole solar business, automated, in one platform — you only show up for the roof, the relationship, and the signature." The benefit is time and certainty: the AI reads the bill, estimates, books the callback, follows up, routes the installer, and assembles the NC trail. You stop being a paper-pusher.
2. **The operator gain:** "One login runs the job — no calculator, spreadsheet, email, calendar, and form-filler in five tabs." Plus a colleague for every role: the owner sees the window, the installer sees the blocker, the customer sees where their solar is.
3. **The trust close:** "And it files the ESB and SEAI paperwork correctly, because it reads the cert against what was typed. Rules run it, AI reads and writes, you sign. Kill the AI and the business still runs." The compliance trail is the proof the automation is trustworthy — stated after the time-back, never before it.

Every public word obeys **truth-pass**: no SMS/WhatsApp claim until built and live, no roof-detection claim until real, no fake statuses, no invented numbers. The product's own honesty architecture is the marketing ethos. This is a moat, not a constraint — installers are lied to by vendors constantly; the "we never pretend" stance is itself a wedge.

## 3. The sales motion (demo IS the pitch)
**The frame: "watch the whole job run itself."** Do not present a feature list — perform the journey a lead takes through the platform, because that journey *is* the product:

1. **The customer uploads a bill** → the AI reads it (no manual entry) → the estimate drops out: savings, grant, payback, from their real day/night usage. *Land it: "That took your customer 30 seconds and your office zero minutes."*
2. **The callback books itself** → customer picks a slot, it's in the diary, the reminder goes. Then show the **automated follow-up** — quiet leads chased, proposal sent, deposit reminded, the installer only hears about what needs a human. *Land it: "This is the 6 hours a week you get back."*
3. **The installer opens one app on site** → today's job, the route, the checklist, the serials — no clipboard, no wrong-turn, no "where's the address." *Land it: "Your fitter lives in this, not in the van with a pen."*
4. **The commissioning gate** → attach the type-test cert → Cross-check now → *"you typed 6.0 kW — the cert reads 5.0."* *Land it: "And when the job's done, the ESB and SEAI trail assembles itself — you're not the paper-pusher anymore."*
5. **The Emergency Stop card** → toggle AI off, show leads still route / surveys book / packs build. *Land it: "We're not a black box. Kill the AI and the business still runs — you stay in every signature."*

The compliance cross-check is moment 4, not the opener. It's the proof the automation is safe — shown after the installer has already felt the time back.

**Objection handling (grounded, from the growth notes):**
- *"OpenSolar does this"* → "They don't do Irish NC6/7 + RECI + SEAI end-to-end, and they don't read the cert and argue with you before it files. That's the bit that gets installers fined."
- *"AI in solar is hype"* → "Ours is the compliance officer, not the copywriter. Kill it and the business runs. Demo the stop button."
- *"Will it send without me?"* → "Never. Every send, sign, file waits on your button."
- *"Can I trust the numbers?"* → "Every figure traces to the bill read and is recorded. Ask the other quote to prove theirs."

**The outbound engine (already built, under-used):** the Renewably CRM holds 1,332 installer contacts. The outreach agent drafts campaigns (capped 40/day, deliverability-protected), Cal approves, replies route to a real inbox. The motion is honest and sharp: *"the agent that booked you this call is what I'm selling you."* The booked call is the proof of the product. Use it.

**The close:** 7-day trial, card captured up front via Stripe subscription (trial_period_days: 7). On signup the tenant is auto-created, the installer drops in their logo + accent (brand themed instantly), pastes the widget embed code on their site (or shares the standalone link — WhatsApp it, QR it on the van), and their first real lead lands attributed. **Full use, day one.** No commitment to a long setup.

## 3b. The three surfaces that make it "one business, not tools"
Beyond the automated journey, three built surfaces are what turn AISolar from a pipeline into an operating system. They are real (verified on disk) and they are the "central nervous system" an installer feels the moment they log in:

- **Customer Intelligence.** Every customer has one profile that gathers the whole truth in one place — bill extraction, survey data, design, proposal, install status — pulled from the same chain, never re-typed. The consultant, the installer, and the owner all see the *same* customer, because there is one record, not five copies in five tools. This is the quiet killer of the "five tabs" problem: the intelligence follows the customer, not the department.

- **The AI Coach — centralised, role-aware.** One AI chat that follows the user across every screen and knows their role. The owner's coach opens with "Call Corrib now — €134k on the table." The installer's coach leads with the blocking NC6 item before the visit. The customer's coach tells them where their solar is and what happens next. It reads the real book (dealIntel — value, days-in-stage, opens, thread tone, NC6 blockers) and answers in the voice of the role. One brain, every department, each hearing only what they need. That is the "colleague for every role" line made concrete.

- **One inbox across departments, and a clean handover.** Sales and field share the *same* conversation thread per customer — the consultant and the installer see the identical message history, so a handover is a context-switch, not a re-brief. The installer picks up exactly where the sales conversation left off. And the handover itself — the commissioning pack, the certs, the warranty, the review ask, the referral card — is assembled as one branded artifact the customer keeps. The job moves from "sold" to "installed" to "handed over" without the information ever being re-typed or lost between people.

These three are why the pitch is "one platform runs the business," not "a better CRM." The intelligence, the coach, and the shared thread mean the *business* is in one place — and the human only enters for the roof, the relationship, and the signature.

## 4. The marketing motion (the wedge, repeated)
**Hero asset — the widget.** The embeddable calculator→lead door is the single best marketing piece we have because it *is* the product in miniature: a customer uploads a bill (or answers chips), sees savings + grant + payback animate in (value before the ask), and the lead lands in the installer's pipeline branded as them. Every installer who embeds it becomes a distribution node. The widget is not a feature — it's the growth engine.

**Content strategy — own the dull stuff.** Irish solar compliance (NC forms, RECI, SEAI, micro-gen bands) is confusing, regulated, and nobody explains it well. Publishing plain-English explainers positions AISolar as the only vendor who actually understands the installer's real risk. This is cheap to produce and impossible for a global tool to localise. Education = demand gen here.

**The flywheel (post-install):** at the 5★ moment, ask for a Google review + drop a referral card *inside the branded handover pack*. Reviews feed the installer's site and the calculator; referrals bring the next lead through the same door. The notes call this "the 360 feeling — what they always wanted." Build it into the pack, not as an afterthought.

**What we do NOT do at launch:** paid breadth plays, marketplace integrations, SMS/WhatsApp channels (until real), status pages, ticketing. Doors, not marketplace. The growth docs are clear that every new channel is just another `source_key` into the same pipe — so we add channels *after* the wedge is proven, not before.

## 5. The launch sequence (from GO_LIVE, with opinion)
The verified order:

1. **Close the one true blocker first — the ESB paper trail.** The vision is built; the *record* of the sealed pack's real submission reference is not yet written (`lead_documents` + `esb_submissions`). Until this lands, we can demo compliance but cannot *prove* it was recorded. This is the difference between "compliant by feature" and "compliant by record" — and it is the foundation of the entire sales wedge. **Do not launch the compliance story until this is wired.** (Your two statutory yes/no's — ESB bands, typed e-signature, NDMG/ACA — unblock this; they are yours to release.)
2. **Deploy functions + your hands items** (edge-fn deploy + secrets, Postmark token + DNS, Vercel domain, A1 auth functions or "build without"). _(The two migrations are already applied + verified live — 8 Aug.)_
3. **Smoke test end-to-end** — door → proposal → deposit (real charge) → install → pack sealed → rows written → handover. Every human button, a real email lands.
4. **Wave 1: Roscommon end-to-end** (the client's three brands) as the proof tenant. Then the grandfather offer rolls to the 32 counties.
5. **The go-live signal is the doors on the live brand sites** — when RI/SI + Saunderson/WideAwake/Solar Roscommon carry the widget and a real lead lands attributed, we are launched. Not a press release. A lead.

**My opinion on sequencing:** the instinct to "revamp the front-end" (GO_LIVE ⑥) is real but secondary. A dated marketing site does not lose the wedge sale if the demo moments land. Ship compliance-recorded + live + the widget first; polish the public pages in Wave 1, not before it. The wedge closes on the demo, not the hero image.

## 6. Pricing & onboarding (the friction killer)
- **7-day trial, card captured, Stripe subscription.** No freemium maze. The trial starts the moment they sign up; past_due = read-only, never data loss.
- **White-label is instant.** Logo + accent + trading name → themes portal/proposal/emails/widget. At trial, outbound sends as "Their Co <notify@platform-domain>" with reply-to their real address — zero DNS. Custom DKIM = post-cohort upgrade, never a blocker.
- **Onboarding is a 10-step checklist, ≤5 min each, on first login.** Sign up → brand → compliance → email → door → booking → pricing dial → practice on the cast → payments → go live. The 5-archetype demo cast is the courseware; the guided walk doubles as cohort training.
- **Support at 10 clients = a WhatsApp group per client + Cal. No ticketing tool.** You know all 10 by name; Stripe handles dunning retries. This is deliberately lean — do not over-build support before there are users to support.

## 7. What we are NOT (keep the story honest)
- Not an AI chatbot product. The AI is a compliance officer and a closer's edge, bounded by human sign-off. The stop button proves it.
- Not a global tool. Irish-statutory-deep by design; that narrowness is the moat.
- Not claiming channels we don't have. Truth-pass is law in product and copy.
- Not finished. The spine is built; the paper trail, A1 door, and several AI lifts are open. The runway sequences them; nothing hidden.

## 8. The one metric that matters
Forget vanity. The number is: **paid tenants → MRR → Cal's draw.** At 10 paying installers (~€1k/mo each) that's ~€10k MRR — the exact finance threshold for drawing income with a trading history. The strategy exists to move that number. Every tactic above is either a door (more leads in) or a wedge (more of those leads close and stay).

The asset is real and unified. The strategy is not to impress with breadth — it is to drive every Irish installer we touch into the one gap nobody else fills, prove it once with a live customer, and let the wedge compound into a business.
